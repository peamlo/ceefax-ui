In a separated mosaic every block shrinks and a gap opens around it, so a solid area becomes a grid of tiles. On teletext sets this was a mode of its own. The Mosaic story Artwork uses it by default, as most portrait art on air did.

Where the gaps come from

Look at one cell, big, with its box dashed: █ (sum 63, contiguous) beside 𜺏 (sum 63, separated).

█𜺏🬋𜹜

In Bedstead, each separated block loses a strip on its left (a sixth of the cell's width) and at its bottom (about a tenth of its height), measured by drawing the glyphs at 100px. Because every cell has its gaps on the same sides, they line up into an even grid across a whole mosaic. You can also see that the middle row of blocks is taller than the top and bottom ones (30 : 40 : 30 of the cell's height). The last pair is the Banner's rule cell: 🬋 and 𜹜, both sum 12.

The gaps are not drawn: they're the parts of the cell the glyph leaves empty, so whatever is behind the mosaic shows through. On a band, that's the band:

Yellow blocks, blue gaps: the two colours a cell can have, which is lesson 7.

The attribute only labels it

Look for a [data-mosaic] rule in mosaic.css: there isn't one. The comment calls the attribute “a presentational marker: the characters carry the look”. So:

charactersattributelooks
separateddata-mosaic="separated"separated ✓
separatednoneseparated, but unlabelled
contiguousdata-mosaic="separated"contiguous: the label is wrong

The attribute tells a reader of the markup, and your own CSS if it wants a hook, what the characters are. The stories keep the two in step: assertSeparated in Mosaic.stories.js fails if any cell's characters disagree with the attribute.

Your turn: separate a row

To make a mosaic separated, keep every cell's sum and rewrite it with the separated formula: U+1CE51 + (sum − 1). That includes ▌ ▐ █: the separated row has no gaps, so their separated twins are ordinary members of it.

Convert the C's middle row, ▌▐██. Cell 1, worked through:

  1. ▌ is the whole left column: sum 1 + 4 + 16 = 21
  2. separated: always sum − 1 = 20
  3. 0x51 = 81, and 81 + 20 = 101
  4. 101 = 6 × 16 + 5 = 0x65 → U+1CE65

Do cells 2–4 the same way (▐, then █ twice, so cells 3 and 4 are the same). Replace the code points in the box: the converted cells turn separated in the logo as you type.
